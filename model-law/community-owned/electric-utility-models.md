# Electric Utility Models — Community-Owned Examples

Municipal and community-owned electric utilities across the U.S., from long-established systems to recent municipalization efforts.

| City | Population | Launched | Summary | Success Metrics | Link |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|----------------|------|
| **Boulder, CO** | ~108,000 | Feasibility study 2011; agreement finalized 2020 | Pursued municipalization to meet carbon reduction goals. Agreement allowed termination if Xcel Energy failed to meet benchmarks. | Carbon emissions reduction benchmarks; local control over energy decisions | [bouldercolorado.gov](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/municipalization) |
| **Chicago, IL** | ~2.7 million | Feasibility study initiated 2019 | Explored acquiring Commonwealth Edison's distribution assets to improve energy efficiency and reliability. | Feasibility study ongoing; metrics not yet available | [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2019/10/3/20896860/chicago-public-electric-utility-feasibility-study-commonwealth-edison) |
| **Sacramento, CA (SMUD)** | ~525,000 | 1946 | Sacramento Municipal Utility District — a well-known example of a successful municipal utility. | High customer satisfaction, renewable energy integration, cost competitiveness | [smud.org](https://www.smud.org/) |
| **Austin, TX** | ~1 million | 1895 | Austin Energy — one of the largest publicly owned utilities in the U.S. | Renewable energy leadership, affordability, energy efficiency programs | [austinenergy.com](https://austinenergy.com/) |
| **Lansing, MI** | ~112,000 | 1885 | Lansing Board of Water & Light — municipally owned utility. | Reliability, affordability, local economic benefits | [lbwl.com](https://www.lbwl.com/) |
| **Winter Park, FL** | ~30,000 | 2005 | Acquired electric utility from Progress Energy to improve reliability and local control. | Improved reliability metrics and customer satisfaction | [cityofwinterpark.org](https://cityofwinterpark.org/departments/electric-utility/) |
